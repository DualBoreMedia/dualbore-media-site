The [FTC shipment guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule) requires support for shipment promises; a made-to-order label does not erase them. Our stricter express-consent approach and evidence gaps are detailed in [the shipment runbook](shipping-compliance.md).

Ohio prohibits deceptive consumer practices, including misleading characteristics, affiliations and delivery claims. The delivery rule's eight-week provisions are not permission to disregard a shorter advertised promise. Do not substitute a different garment without consent. Sources: [ORC 1345.02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1345.02), [OAC 109:4-3-09](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-109:4-3-09). Have Ohio counsel review final sales/return language and the actual merchant practices, including mandatory remedies; do not claim a voluntary exchange window extinguishes them.

## Privacy, security and email

- Ohio breach-response duties require a fact-specific incident assessment and timely notification when statutory conditions apply; the statute generally specifies an outer 45-day limit subject to its qualifications. Establish a response owner and evidence-preserving process, not an unconditional promise that every technical incident is a reportable breach. [ORC 1349.19](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1349.19).
- Determine audience, data volume, jurisdictions and actual child-directed/knowledge facts before claiming an exemption from broader state privacy or children's rules. Pit-bike content alone does not establish COPPA applicability or exemption. Do not invent an age gate or claim no children ever visit. Review actual practices with counsel using the [FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).
- No order automatically grants marketing consent. Future Drop Alert permission must be explicit, optional and unchecked. Keep suppression/unsubscribe records and transactional messages separate. Commercial email needs compliant headers/subjects, required identification/address and a working opt-out honored within ten business days. Do not insert promotions into support or order mail assuming it is exempt. No mailing-address guess or email activation is made here. [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).
- Current policies disclose meaningful present flows without publishing secrets or customer records. Future provider regions, contracts, database processor, retention and authentication/telemetry configuration need verification before activation. Browser/security checks are evidence of tested behavior, not a guarantee of perfect security.

## Apparel and advertising

1. Obtain actual Gildan blank model/color/size specifications, invoices and labels. Verify fiber content, country of origin and responsible manufacturer/marketer identification before publishing claims or replacing labels. Online textile descriptions also need appropriate origin disclosure; a brand name is not proof of a model or material. Preserve original labels unless a legally compliant replacement is verified. No fabricated GSM, material, measurements or country claims are introduced. [FTC textile labeling guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/threading-your-way-through-labeling-requirements-under-textile-wool-acts).
2. Confirm care instructions remain safe after the specific print/transfer process. Do not copy generic laundering guarantees without a reasonable basis. [FTC Care Labeling Rule guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/clothes-captioning-complying-care-labeling-rule).
3. Applying artwork and packing in Ohio does not substantiate unqualified US manufacture of the blank garment. Use qualified decoration/packing wording and verify origin separately. [FTC Made in USA standard](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-made-usa-standard).
4. Verify applicable flammability compliance and supplier documentation for the finished apparel. Do not assume a fabric/testing exemption from a photo. Confirm whether any goods are intended as children's products; that can bring additional certification, lead/decoration, tracking and testing requirements. No children's or sleepwear compliance claim is made. [CPSC clothing guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Clothing-OLD).
5. Review actual sponsor, affiliate and free-product relationships and disclose material connections clearly where relevant. Do not fabricate reviews, endorsements, stock urgency, performance or product quality claims. None was established by the provided facts. [FTC endorsement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking).

## Intellectual property and attorney review

The site uses authentic channel assets and merchandise photographs, but possession of a file is not proof of commercial artwork rights. Obtain the creator's assignment/license, source-photo rights and evidence for each design and brand emblem. Vehicle references in editorial descriptions and brand marks on sold apparel present different factual issues. Public Terms disclaims manufacturer sponsorship; that does not clear infringement. Counsel should review the BMW-related designs, visible vehicle badges, Suzuki silhouette, DualBore name/emblem and any future third-party logos before sales. Trademark confusion and copyright fair use need fact-specific analysis; there is no automatic fan-art exception. Sources: [USPTO infringement guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/page/about-trademark-infringement), [US Copyright Office fair use](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/).

Include the existing website previews in that rights review. A non-selling preview is not automatically exempt from copyright or trademark analysis; permission for the public display and permission for apparel reproduction both need consideration.
